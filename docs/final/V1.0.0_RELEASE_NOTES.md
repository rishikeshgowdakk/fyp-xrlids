## 1. Project Scope & Operational Boundaries

XRL-IDARS is an autonomous cyber defense research platform designed to investigate whether explainable reinforcement learning paired with strict safety gating and atomic rollback can enable robust, cost-effective intrusion mitigation.

```text
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                                  OPERATIONAL BOUNDARY                                   |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| [✓] Offline Research Benchmarking           --> SCIENTIFICALLY VALIDATED & FROZEN       |
| [✓] Isolated Laboratory Controlled Response  --> EXPERIMENTALLY VALIDATED & AUDITED      |
| [✗] Enterprise Production Deployment        --> STRICTLY UNVALIDATED & OUT OF SCOPE     |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

- **Offline Research:** Scientifically validated across 20 raw CSV datasets (2,830,743 raw CIC-IDS2017 rows, 16,233,002 CSE-CIC-IDS2018 rows, 257,673 UNSW-NB15 rows) under strict zero-leakage deduplication (Policy A).
- **Isolated Laboratory Enforcement:** Evaluated strictly inside Linux network namespaces (`10.200.1.0/24`) with dual-mode topology (attacker vs victim). Host network interfaces, routing tables, and firewall rules remained cryptographically unchanged (`pre == post` SHA-256 match).
- **Production Deployment:** Strictly unvalidated. The system is NOT approved or certified for direct deployment onto live enterprise or campus networks.

## 2. Scientific Status

- **Phase 1 (Data, Baselines & Transferability):** **SCIENTIFICALLY FROZEN**.
  - Established canonical column normalization and R10 10-feature transport contract (`EXP-P1-CIC2017-R10-001`, `EXP-P1-CSE2018-R10-MULTI-001`).
  - Proved that naive random splitting leaks up to 34.5% duplicate feature vectors across splits; Policy A feature deduplication and conflict rejection eliminated 100% of duplicate leakage.
  - Demonstrated that tabular Random Forest (F1 0.8861–0.9512) and sequence LSTM (F1 0.8637–0.9633) form complementary inductive biases. Validation-tuned convex score fusion delivered statistically significant F1 improvements across all datasets (CIC-IDS2017 F1 = 0.9745, CSE-CIC-IDS2018 F1 = 0.9603, UNSW-NB15 F1 = 0.8970).
  - Evaluated unadapted cross-dataset transfer across 6 directions, observing catastrophic domain collapse (52% to 95% F1 drop) due to MTU, timeout, and distribution shift (RQ5).
- **Phase 2 (Autonomous Response & Explainability):** **SCIENTIFICALLY FROZEN** at commit `11f32f658cde525300f8bc46088a0143e8329c21`.
  - Benchmark ladder evaluated across 4 deterministic baselines on 142,346 validation flows (`EXP-P2A-BASELINES-001`), establishing Baseline 2 (Two-Tier $\tau=0.40/0.75$) as the lowest-cost baseline.
  - Dueling DQN with Prioritized Experience Replay trained across 3 cost regimes (`EXP-P2B-DQN-001`).
  - Evaluated once on 355,865 held-out test flows ($D_{\text{pol\_test}}$, `EXP-P2-FINAL-TEST-001`). Paired bootstrap confirmed statistically significant cost reductions vs Baseline 2 (+11.29% Balanced, +14.10% Aggressive, $p < 0.001$), but failed the pre-registered practical significance criterion ($\ge 15.0\%$).
  - Dual-layer explainability achieved 100.00% audit coverage across 28,167 non-ALLOW interventions with 100% safety gate compliance (0 unauthorized critical infrastructure isolations, `EXP-P2E-EXPLAINABILITY-001`).

## 4. Major Empirical Findings

### Positive Findings (Validated Strengths)
1. **Score Fusion Outperforms Single Models:** Convex combination of Random Forest and Supervised LSTM consistently achieved superior F1 scores and reduced false positive rates across all three benchmark datasets ($p < 0.001$).
2. **Policy A Completely Eliminates Duplicate Leakage:** Feature deduplication and label-conflict rejection resolved the ubiquitous data leakage prevalent in published IDS benchmarks.
3. **Dual-Layer Explainability Achieves Complete Auditability:** 100% of non-ALLOW actions are traced by perceptual TreeSHAP attributions and policy Q-value decompositions.
4. **Deterministic Safety Gate Guarantees Zero Invariant Violations:** Across all evaluated phases and 10,000-flow stress workloads, zero unauthorized critical infrastructure isolations occurred.
5. **Atomic Rollback and Netns Isolation Prevent Host Damage:** 100% of compensation rollbacks succeeded, and cryptographic hashes proved zero mutations to the host networking stack.

### Negative & Scoped Findings (Crucial Academic Insights)
1. **Unadapted Cross-Dataset Transfer Fails Catastrophically:** Detectors trained on CIC-IDS2017 suffered up to 95% F1 collapse on CSE-CIC-IDS2018. Tabular features do not transfer out-of-domain without target adaptation.
2. **DQN Practical Target Not Met:** Although DQN achieved statistically significant cost reductions (+11.29% and +14.10%, $p < 0.001$) on held-out test data, it failed the pre-registered operational threshold ($\ge 15.0\%$).
3. **4-Action DQN Suffers High Chattering:** The 4-action space (including `RATE_LIMIT`) exhibited unstable oscillation ($\text{ACI} = 0.0214 > 0.01$), whereas the 3-action space remained stable ($\text{ACI} = 0.0019$).
4. **Detector Latency Dominates Pipeline:** Neural model inference (47.6 ms/flow) represents the primary pipeline bottleneck. High-throughput packet ingestion requires application-level backpressure shedding during line-speed bursts.
5. **No Ground Truth on Live Traffic:** Operational accuracy cannot be evaluated on unlabelled live streams without independent capture truth.

## 5. Known Limitations

1. **Feature Set Portability:** The R10 feature set relies on bidirectional flow statistical summaries that cannot be extracted from datasets missing transport-level metrics (e.g., UNSW-NB15 required fallback to 4 features).
2. **Namespace-Bound Enforcement:** Real network enforcement mechanisms were evaluated exclusively in Linux network namespaces with virtual veth interfaces, not physical enterprise ASICs, SDN switches, or hardware firewalls.
3. **Sequence Alignment:** LSTM sequences ($T=5$) reflect flow arrival order within sample partitions, not continuous physical wall-clock packet timelines.
4. **Single-Seed Headline Baseline:** Frozen headline artifacts reflect single-seed (seed=42) execution. *Update (2026-10-17):* a full 3-seed robustness sweep (seeds 42/123/456) is now completed for all three benchmarks — seed 42 reproduces the frozen artifacts exactly and headline metrics are seed-stable (mean ± std in `reports/generated/multi_seed_report.md`).
